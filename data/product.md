# Product

*The foundation document. Every draft, outreach, and channel decision derives
from what's written here — fill it in before anything else (the setup skill
walks you through it). Replace each ✏️ block; delete the instruction lines when
done. Marketing skill packs read this context (exported to
`.agents/product-marketing.md` by the setup skill), so the more honest and
specific it is, the less every skill has to re-ask. Log every material change in
the Changelog section at the bottom.*

## What is it

*One-liner first (the sentence you'd say at a party), then a short paragraph:
what the product does, for whom, and what changes for them when it works.*

✏️

## Ideal customer profile

*Who exactly — role, context, pains. Not "developers": which developers, at what
moment of what struggle. Add where they actually hang out (communities,
newsletters, people they follow); this drives venue pruning. End with
**disqualifiers**: who you deliberately don't serve, so prospecting and ads know
what to skip.*

✏️

## Personas

*2–3 buying personas: name the role, what they're responsible for, what a win
looks like for them, what they fear. Include one **anti-persona** — the person
who looks like a fit but always churns or misuses the product.*

✏️

## Positioning

*Fill the classic frame honestly — the "unlike" line is the one that does the
work:*

> For **[who]** struggling with **[problem]**, **[product]** is a **[category]**
> that **[key benefit]**. Unlike **[main alternative]**, it **[sharpest
> differentiator]**.

✏️

## Competitive landscape

*The 2–4 alternatives your customers actually weigh (including "do nothing" and
spreadsheets if honest). One line each: what they're better at, where they fall
short. Keep the deep profiles in [Competitors](competitors) — link them from
here.*

✏️

## Messaging pillars

*Define 3–5 recurring themes you want every piece of content to reinforce. One
per line: `slug — one-line description`. Use the slug in the `pillar`
frontmatter field of posts, so you can query coverage per pillar later.*

✏️

## Objections

*The recurring reasons people don't buy, each with your best response and the
proof that backs it: `objection → response → proof`. Feed this from real
conversations — sales calls, community threads, cancel reasons. Copy and ads
skills mine this list.*

✏️

## Customer language

*Verbatim phrases customers use for the problem and the outcome — their words,
not yours. Pull from reviews, support tickets, community threads, and your
research records (deliberate research lives in [Interviews](interviews);
spontaneous quotes about your product live in [Mentions](mentions) — this
section distills the best of both plus problem-space language). Headlines get
written from this section.*

✏️

## Switching forces

*Jobs-to-be-done framing of why someone moves: what **pushes** them off the
current solution, what **pulls** them to yours, what **habit** keeps them where
they are, what **anxiety** blocks the switch. One or two honest lines each.*

✏️

## Proof

*Everything that makes claims credible: numbers, testimonials, named users,
benchmarks, comparisons, demo links. Tag each entry with who it's most relevant
to (persona or industry) so drafts can pick the right proof. Keep it current.*

✏️

## Changelog

*Newest first: `YYYY-MM-DD — what changed and why`. Positioning drift is
invisible without this; skills use it to notice when their cached understanding
is stale.*
